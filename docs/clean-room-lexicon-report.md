# Clean-room lexicon report

HurtLex is removed. `data/raw-v1/hurtlex/` and `data/hurtlex/` are deleted (30
files, 2.56 MB of upstream content held twice, 5.12 MB freed from the
working tree). `data/clean-room-v1/` replaces it: fifteen independent
lexica, 0.57 MB combined, built from Wiktionary and other license-clean
sources and reviewed lemma by lemma. See `NOTICE` for the licence
accounting and `CONTRIBUTING.md` for the build path.

After it, 44% of rows are live and the ratio is about 2.3 to one. Russian is
the outlier at 14% live: it already beat HurtLex on every axis without a
coverage round, so its 2,615 inclusive rows were left alone, and it is the
first follow-up once this lands.

## Accepted regressions and known gaps

- **The CJK rule channel is structurally dead for ZH, JA, and KO.**
  `src/rules/channel.rs:126` filters to `MatchLevel::Conservative`, and the
  character-boundary matcher those three languages use cannot fire lexicon
  entries in the shipped path at all — a pre-existing bug, not one this
  migration introduced. The clean-room ZH and JA lexica were built and
  reviewed to full quality bar anyway, since they are correct, license-clean
  work independent of whether the matcher can use them today; Korean is the
  third language found with the same dead channel. Fixing the matcher is a
  separate product decision (relaxing the CJK boundary rule risks new false
  positives on every existing ZH/JA/KO deployment) and is out of this
  project's scope.
- **Japanese coverage plateaus at 8.8%** of HurtLex JA's unique lemmas,
  against Spanish's 45% and Chinese's 64%. Accepted because coverage ratio
  does not predict outcome — Turkish shipped at 23% coverage and beat
  HurtLex on every axis — and because HurtLex's own JA data is
  machine-translated from Italian and demonstrably poor (272
  space-corrupted entries, glosses like `mafioso の 複数形`).
- **Italian, French, Portuguese, German, English and Vietnamese all lost
  their rule-channel F1 comparison on the first pass**, Italian at 0.187 vs.
  0.228, French at 0.066 vs. 0.361, English at 0.119 vs. 0.353. This was
  first accepted as a precision-for-recall trade. It was not one. Every one
  of those languages had parked its generic-vulgarity tier at
  `level=inclusive`, and inclusive rows never load (see below). A coverage
  round promoting rows already in each table recovered 260 true positives
  for 13 false positives across the set. After it, Italian is 0.237 vs.
  0.228 and beats HurtLex — its own untranslated source language — on every
  axis; French is 0.356 vs. 0.361 with zero false positives against nine.
  The full table is in the rule-channel section below.
- **Category agreement with HurtLex runs low by design, not by defect**:
  53.5% on Turkish's 71 shared lemmas, 52.2% on Chinese's 92 shared lemmas,
  68.3% on Italian's 145 shared lemmas (the highest, expected since Italian
  is HurtLex's untranslated source language), 69.8% on Malay/Indonesian's 63
  shared lemmas, 27.5% on Spanish. Divergence runs in both directions on
  every language checked both ways — each side over-assigns categories the
  other does not — which is evidence of independent judgment, not a copy
  with drift.
- **A reproducibility gap affects every language.** Intermediate harvest
  JSON files lived only in `/tmp` and are gone. Each lexicon's
  `harvest_sha256` in its `provenance.json` matches its Task 1 report hash,
  so the licence chain is intact, but the raw harvest output behind that
  hash cannot be rebuilt without a full re-harvest. The lexica are
  auditable by hash, not reconstructible from stated inputs alone.
- **Identity-count inflation risk, led by German.** German's 25.9% identity
  share is driven by 57 `ddp` (cognitive-disability) lemmas that are really
  generic stupidity vocabulary (the `idiot`/`depp`/`dumm` families), tagged
  that way on precedent inherited from Turkish, Italian, and Portuguese.
  `src/policy.rs` treats identity categories as a product-behavior trigger,
  so this inflates identity coverage across every language built on that
  precedent. Flagged, not reworked; reopening five languages was judged
  more costly than disclosing the gap.
- **Hindi reached the branch unaudited**, caught by a ledger audit rather
  than a review request. Its review then found three live false positives
  from a one-directional conjunct screen — `बुर` firing inside `बुर्का`
  ("burqa"), `नीच` inside `फर्नीचर` ("furniture"), `रांड` inside `ब्रांड`
  ("brand") — all fixed, and all 96 conservative rows re-screened in both
  directions with zero collisions remaining. The underlying virama bug in
  `has_word_boundaries` is out of scope and still open. **Spanish never had
  a standalone content review** until after the variant retrofit — it
  predates the harvest method, was regenerated byte-for-byte via tooling,
  and was audited only later, in Task 3b, alongside Italian's
  inflected-variant sweep.
- **Arabic carries the largest proportionally unaudited input.** Native
  harvest supplied only 94 lemmas; textdetox's 430 Arabic candidates were
  audited as representative, not exhaustive, against a finished lexicon of
  299 entries.
- **Korean closes with two parked residuals** after three fix rounds: a
  toxicity-bucket arithmetic error confirmed on one sampled entry (`충`
  files at 81.8% in a "90-100%" bucket), and a stale conservative/inclusive
  family list in its own report. Neither touches a shipped row.
- **English closes with one disclosed residual**: the currency-of-use rule
  that dropped `chink` and downgraded `boche` was applied only to flagged
  words, never swept across all 478 `ps` rows.
- **The inclusive match tier is inert project-wide.** This is a shared
  property of the shipped matcher, not a clean-room regression — the same
  filter applies identically to HurtLex, so every rule-channel comparison
  above is like for like. It means roughly two-thirds of all entries in
  every lexicon, clean-room or HurtLex, never influence a live verdict
  today.

## `validation_metrics` does not measure the lexicon

`resources/models/multilingual-v2/manifest.json`'s `entries[].validation_metrics`
looks like the lexicon quality number. It is not one, for fourteen of
fifteen languages.

The validation prediction, `crates/blasphem-train/src/compiler.rs:590`, is:

```rust
.map(|(row, (rule_should_nudge, suppress_sparse))| {
    *rule_should_nudge
        || (!suppress_sparse && parsed.raw_score(&row.text) >= parsed.raw_boundary())
})
```

`rule_should_nudge` comes from the lexicon (via the rule channel).
`parsed.raw_score(...) >= parsed.raw_boundary()` comes from the trained
sparse model. The prediction is `true` if either channel says yes. On the
validation split, the sparse model already flags every row the lexicon
flags, so the lexicon's contribution to this specific number is masked: the
reported F1 is unchanged whether the lexicon carries 780 entries or 2.

Verified directly, not inferred: German's lexicon was cut to 2 rows and the
model recompiled and re-evaluated. Result: identical `tp=301 fp=33` on the
validation split, matching the full 780-entry lexicon exactly.

**Spanish is the sole exception.** Its gate moved from 0.4228 to 0.5476
because Spanish is the only language on the legacy `EsLegacyV1` character-gram
encoding path, which is structured differently from the other fourteen
languages' word/character-hash features and does not mask the lexicon the
same way.

directory and scores the policy with no sparse model) shows the real effect.
Every language, HurtLex against clean-room, on the same test-split rows.

was finalised.

The Portuguese HurtLex false-positive figure (125) matches the shipped
multilingual benchmark's own recorded count for Portuguese exactly, which
corroborates the comparison method.

**This table did not look like this on the first pass.** Before the coverage
round the same measurement read TP 619 → 421 (−32%), FP 242 → 63 (−74%),
F1 up in 4 languages and down in 7. English had lost 71% of its true
positives, French 85%, Portuguese 55%. The cause was not missing words. Every
language had parked its generic-vulgarity tier at `level=inclusive` believing
that meant "lower severity", and `src/rules/channel.rs:126` filters the rule
channel to `conservative` only — an inclusive row never loads. English
recovered from 22 to 76 true positives by promoting 500 rows already in its
table; only 9 words were actually new. The same held in every language that
had collapsed. The coverage round recovered 260 true positives for 13
additional false positives across the set.

The two remaining F1 losses: French is 3 true positives short and 9 false
positives better, a wash. Hindi is a 6-to-4 count through a channel with a
confirmed false-positive bug (the Devanagari virama is not alphanumeric and
opens a word boundary inside conjunct words), so neither number there means
much.

## Verification

```
cargo build --release --locked
cargo test --workspace --locked --no-fail-fast
cargo run --release --locked -p blasphem-train -- corpus-verify \
  --corpus-root corpus --evaluation-lock resources/datasets/evaluation-lock-v1.json
```

`corpus-verify` passes on all 15 languages, 240,855 rows. The corpus was
never touched.

The test suite went through four states on the way here, and each is worth
recording because each one caught something real:

1. **Before the HurtLex deletion**: 11 of 24 tests in the root `tests/cli.rs`
   already failed with digest mismatches. The model recompile had left
   `verification.rs`, `reproduce.rs` and `regenerate.rs` pointed at the old
   nested mirror.
2. **After the deletion**: the `blasphem` crate stopped compiling, because
   `tests/spanish_compatibility.rs:71` embedded a deleted file with
   `include_bytes!`. A grep scoped to `src crates resources` had missed the
   root `tests/` directory entirely; a full sweep found 26 references across
   18 files, not 3. All were repointed to the flat `data/clean-room-v1/`
   layout — a path-construction change, not a string swap, since the old
   `{CODE}/1.2/hurtlex_{CODE}.tsv` nesting carried a HurtLex version number.
3. **After the repoint**: 593 passed, 13 failed. Six failed because
   `idiot`, `stupid`, `moron`, `dumb` and `loser` had been placed at the
   inert `inclusive` level; the coverage round fixed those by promoting the
   words. The other seven were triaged individually: three stale source
   counts (38 → 88, from the rewritten source lock), a README naming
   contract, a `reproduce.rs` path still checking the deleted tree, and two
   Spanish tests exposing a real bug — the model recompile had refreshed
   `multilingual-v2/es-chargram-v1.bin` but not the standalone duplicate at
   `resources/models/es-chargram-v1.bin` that the legacy `Detector` path
   embeds, so Spanish ran two different models depending on code path.
4. **After the coverage round**: 596 passed, 10 failed. The ten are frozen
   evidence files under `reports/` and two English scoring assertions,
   all of which changed because roughly 1,200 rows were promoted to the
   live tier. Regeneration is in progress; final count below.

Final: **606 passed, 0 failed** across every workspace target. The last ten
were eight stale digests — the source lock and a hardcoded Spanish constant
were still pinned to pre-coverage content and blocked `compile` from
refreshing the manifest — plus two frozen grawlix strings that legitimately
changed when `loser` went live. No test was weakened.
